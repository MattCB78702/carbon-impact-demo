# Carbon Impact — Demo talk track (New Belgium Brewing)

**Live demo:** https://mattcb78702.github.io/carbon-impact-demo/
**Audience:** New Belgium (Sayali owns the deal; Pankaj sent the June 2026 catalogue)
**The user in the room:** the sustainability buyer. Everything here is built for them.

**The argument in one breath:** buying credits is not the hard part. Defending the purchase to legal, finance, marketing, and every employee at a bar is. Carbon Impact does four things: it educates, it helps you select, it benchmarks you, and it gives you what you need to communicate before and after the purchase. CarbonBetter still does the diligence. The platform makes that work legible.

**If asked what the platform does versus what we do:** the platform never claims to assess a project. We assess projects. The platform shows the assessment in a form you can take to your board, and keeps the evidence attached to every claim you make.

---

## 1. Your Footprint (open here)

Start with their number, not our product.

- "286,000 tonnes, and 87% of it is your supply chain. Glass, aluminium, barley, refrigeration at retail."
- Then the coverage table, which is the honesty moment: the brand program is fully covered, the portfolio is about a quarter of Scope 1 and 2, and 3.5% of the total. "Credits are the last mile, not the plan."
- Close the tab on the 2030 line: a company-wide neutral commitment implies a credit program an order of magnitude bigger than today's. "That is why it is worth settling quality and claims discipline now, while the volumes are small."

## 2. Quality and Co-benefits

This is the education tab, and it is the answer to "how do I know any of this is real."

- Walk the five questions: additionality, quantification, permanence, leakage, safeguards. Each has a plain-English version underneath.
- The avoidance versus removal section is the one a journalist will test them on.
- Point at the co-benefit rule: claim it only where the documentation evidences it. "This is the discipline that keeps you out of trouble."

## 3. Explore Credits

The proof that the recommendation was a choice, not a pitch.

- All 26 projects from their real catalogue. Filter live in front of them: click **In a value-chain state** and watch 26 drop to 7.
- Open a row. Real methodology, verifier, quality signals, volume.
- "Six of these are in your portfolio. The other twenty are why we can tell you what we did not pick."

## 4. Your Portfolio

Leads with criteria, then the projects, because that is the order the decision was actually made in.

- The criteria table has a third column: what each criterion ruled out. "Geography alone removed most of the market by volume."
- Then six projects, each labelled with the role it plays and the criteria it satisfies.
- Finish on **What was set aside**, especially the HFC sleeve: "your current program is $2 to $3 a tonne and concentrated in one project type. We kept it as an option, but it gives marketing nothing new to say."

## 5. Benchmark

The most differentiated screen. Nobody else can build this.

- "You are 7th of 74 beverage buyers by volume retired. Median is 693 tonnes. You are at 15,597."
- Then the punchline: the sector buys 60% forestry. They buy industrial gas. "That has been defensible on quality, because metered destruction beats modelled forest carbon. It is weaker on story, and it leaves you open to the criticism that your credits have nothing to do with beer."
- BrewDog's 450,000 tonnes came from Darkwoods, which is in their catalogue. Use it as a cautionary tale, not a target.

## 6. Share

Two moments, two audiences.

- **Before the purchase:** exec one-pager, finance pack, legal pack. "This is how it gets approved."
- **After the purchase:** marketing kit, employee page, retirement records. "This is how it gets explained without overstating it."
- Best single click: **Legal pack → the claim that is not approved.** "The tool knows what you cannot say. That is what keeps marketing and legal out of each other's inboxes."
- Second best: **Retirement records.** Their three real public retirements, with the purpose text naming Fat Tire and Mountain Time.

## Q&A

- **"Is this live software?"** A working preview on your real registry history and our real catalogue. The product ships with the engagement.
- **"Where do the prices come from?"** Bands here. Firm quotes are in your catalogue. (Never quote exact prices from the public link.)
- **"Where does the footprint data come from?"** Public reporting. The live workspace runs on your own inventory.
- **"Can the platform do the diligence for us?"** No, and it should not pretend to. We do the diligence. The platform is where you see it, interrogate it, and hand it to your colleagues.
- **"The design?"** It is a working preview. A design pass comes before anything goes to a customer.

## Known gaps, if pushed

Plan-and-decide process management (contracts, approvals, payment schedules) is deliberately absent. This is a tool to understand and communicate, not to run procurement. If a prospect wants procurement workflow, that is a separate conversation and a separate product.
